Backend view · where each change lands

High-level architecture of the shop and the checkout request path, then every proposed change mapped to the backend parts it touches. The site says "Powered by nopCommerce", so the modules follow that platform's standard design; internal details are inferred, not confirmed with the team.

Checkout request path (numbered)
  1. 1 Nour clicks Checkout or Confirm in her browser.
  2. 2 The web server passes the request to the application server.
  3. 3 Cart & order totals reads her cart, prices and fee settings from the database.
  4. 4 Payment would call a gateway; in the demo no gateway is connected.
  5. 5 Shipping would ask a carrier for rates; today rates are fixed in settings.
  6. 6 The page comes back with the total: this is where 10.00 becomes 17.00.

Red lines inside each module list the evidence IDs found there. Dashed boxes are services the demo doesn't use.

How to read the to-be
  • Green = a part that changes. The green numbers are the change IDs in the table below.
  • Cart & order totals and Payment carry the most changes: that's the 10.00 → 17.00 fix.
  • Carrier rates (C7) is the only new outside service, needed for real delivery dates. It can start as fixed rules per area and connect a carrier later.
  • The Database gets one new kind of data: delivery dates per product or area.

Quick wins (no code)
Changes 1, 2, 6, 7 are content, settings or small page edits. They fix trust gaps in days, and the admin can do change 1 alone.
The real work
Changes 3 and 4 need back-end logic and new data. They fix the costliest exit (E17), so they lead Sprint 1 or 2.
Why a PM draws this
It turns "fix checkout" into a question engineering can size: which modules, what type of change, what new data. That's the input for sprint planning.
Where I'd adjust your model. Your example diagram is a good shape (client → load balancer → app server → databases), and the PM's version stops there. The PM doesn't choose the database or the framework; they show which boxes a change touches and ask engineering to confirm the size.
