2. Purpose of the evaluation

Find what stops a first-time buyer from finishing a purchase, and rank the fixes by their effect on checkout completion. The primary persona is Nour, a doubtful first-time buyer who usually shops on Amazon.

Aspects analysed: usability, trust and content, cost and delivery transparency, navigation, and clarity. Not analysed this round: accessibility, performance, mobile layout.

4. User testing and feedback summary

Test methodology
Heuristic evaluation plus a goal-anchored expert review (each screen scored 1–5 for how well it serves checkout completion).
Number of users tested
0
No users were reachable, and the site is a test shop with no real customers.
Tasks walked
T1 Find a laptop with search · T2 Judge a product page · T3 Guest checkout with cash on delivery and in-store pickup · T4 Guest checkout with ground shipping.
Key observations
A flat 7.00 fee appears only at the last step (+70% on our 10.00 test order); no delivery date appears anywhere; placeholder text on four pages; payment options only at step 4.
Common user feedback: not collected. In place of quotes, the annotated screens show what the persona would likely think at each step; these lines are hypotheses, not user words. Next round: an exit survey on cart and checkout, and a 5-user usability test.

Our edge: guest checkout
None of the three lets you buy without an account. Demo Web Shop already does. Making guest checkout obvious turns a weakness (E10) into the one thing it does better than Amazon, Noon and Jumia for a buyer like Nour.
Table stakes we miss
All three show a delivery date, often with a "order within…" countdown, and a returns promise on the product page. Demo Web Shop shows neither. This is the gap that sends Nour to compare.
The fee is normal; hiding it isn't
Amazon and Noon charge 10–12 EGP for cash on delivery too, and Amazon even flags "electronic payment only" on the product page. The fix is to show fees and payment limits early, not necessarily to remove them.
Even leaders slip
Jumia's cart shows only the item total, after its product page said home delivery costs 30 EGP. Showing the real total in the cart is a chance to do better than a market leader.

8. Conclusion

Demo Web Shop's checkout works, but it hides what a doubtful buyer needs most: the real total, the delivery date and a returns promise. The single most damaging moment is the confirm page, where a 7.00 fee the buyer never chose appears after every form is filled (+70% on our 10.00 test order).

Most trust problems are content and settings, fixable in days. The cost and delivery problems need back-end work but remove the costliest exit. Improving these should raise checkout completion; real users and funnel data must confirm it.
