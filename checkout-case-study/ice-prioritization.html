1. Why impact × effort was not enough

Our first ranking used impact and effort only. Gilad's critique: both are guesses, and we guess in one direction. This is the planning fallacy: we underestimate effort and overestimate impact. In his experience, at best 1 in 3 ideas actually moves the metric. A chart of guesses looks precise but isn't.

The fix is to add a third factor that says how much to trust the first two: Confidence, scored from evidence, not from feeling.

2. The Confidence Meter

Each piece of evidence earns points. Opinions are nearly worthless; tests and real launch numbers are worth thousands. The total puts the idea in a band.

Up I4 trust content → 1st
Cheap and backed by two rivals. Easy, sure, and impactful: the clearest ICE winner.
Up I2 delivery promise → 2nd
Every rival shows a date. Market data lifts it above the quick wins despite a sprint of work.
Down I3 guest checkout → 3rd
Still cheap, but no rival does it, so only our walkthrough backs it. Our edge is also our biggest unknown.
Down I1 real total → 5th
Highest impact, but one walkthrough is all we have. It would need C ≥ 1.15 (market-data level) to pass I3. That is a reason to test it cheaply, not to drop it.

7. How to raise confidence (GIST step-projects)

Gilad's answer to low confidence is not more debate but a cheap step that produces evidence. Each step adds points on the meter.

I1

Real total in the cart

Highest impact, lowest proof. Validate before the sprint, don't drop it.
Back-of-envelopeCount orders whose final total differs from the cart total (admin panel, C5).+5
DataLook at exits on the confirm page in the store's analytics, if there is any traffic.+500

Where I'd adjust your model. In a real company, ICE does not pick the roadmap; it picks what to learn next. Our confidence tops out at 1.5 / 10 because no user has touched any idea. So the sprint plan should mix two things: ship the cheap, well-backed changes (I4, I3) and run the tests that raise confidence on the big bets (I1, I2). Re-score after each test; the ranking is meant to move.
