and the 3-release plan to fix it

A product review of Demo Web Shop, a live demo online store: mapped the checkout, found where a doubtful first-time buyer gives up, benchmarked Amazon, Noon and Jumia, asked an independent AI review tool for a second opinion, then turned it into prioritized stories, 3 sprints and 3 safe releases.

01

Goal, user and job to be done

Why checkout, and for whom
Business goal
More completed orders from visitors the shop already has.
North star
Checkout completion rate: carts that become orders.
Revenue logic
Orders = visitors × add-to-cart × checkout completion. The third is money already in the cart.
N
Nour, doubtful first-time buyer (example persona)
Shops on Amazon; found a lower price here but has never heard of this shop; won't register for one purchase.
Needs: real total, delivery date, accepted payment, returns, proof the shop is real.
Leaves when: a cost appears late, a form blocks her, the shop looks unfinished.
When I find a product cheaper than on Amazon but I don't know this shop, help me see the full price, delivery date and returns before I commit, so I can buy without feeling tricked.
Constraint
No access to users or analytics: evidence from the live site, screenshots, competitors and an independent AI review. Stated openly throughout.
💡
My thinking The brief offered "a user who wants to buy". I narrowed it to a buyer who doesn't trust the shop yet, because that person feels every gap: price, delivery, returns.

True problem
The shop asks a stranger to commit before showing what she needs to trust it, then changes the price at the last step.

04

What is it worth, and which fixes first?

Back-of-envelope value, then ICE = Impact × Confidence × Ease
Illustrative value (example numbers)
1,000 checkouts started a month × 40% completion = 400 orders
+5 points completion → +50 orders × 1,000 EGP = +50,000 EGP a month
In scope
Product page → thank-you, guest buyers.
Out of scope
Business accounts, search filters, carrier integration.
Why now
Every week the surprise stays, buyers who already chose a product are lost; the quick fixes cost days.
Key call: the "real total" fix has the biggest impact but the least proof, so it is tested with buyers first and built in sprint 3.
💡
My thinking Impact × effort alone overrates big ideas. Adding confidence from evidence moved the "real total" fix from 3rd to 5th, so we test it before we build it.

How we'll know it worked, and what could go wrong

North star, one success metric per release, guardrails
Risk
Buyer tests show the surprise matters less → fix shrinks to the no-fee default.
Risk
Delivery rules take longer → "Arrives by date" first, countdown later.
Risk
No traffic for an A/B test → before/after on the sprint-1 baseline.
💡
My thinking One north star, one success metric per release, and guardrails written before launch, while nobody is attached to the feature.

08

What I learned

And what I would do next with real access
Lessons
  • Writing stories before the evidence made the learning visible.
  • A second opinion is worth it: the double-check caught that my walkthrough used a saved address, hiding a whole form.
  • Confidence from evidence changes priorities more than impact guesses do.
  • A release plan is a plan for being wrong safely.
Next, with real access
  • Funnel tracking per step for a true baseline.
  • Usability test with 5–10 first-time buyers on the real-total prototype.
  • Redo the walkthrough as a true first-time guest.
  • Re-run the AI review after each release to show the score move.
Honest limit
No real users were interviewed. Every finding is expert or AI opinion until the sprint-1 buyer test and funnel data. Persona, team, dates and money figures are examples.
