Why first-time buyers abandon checkout, and the 3-release plan to fix it

A product review of Demo Web Shop, a live demo online store. I mapped the checkout, found where a doubtful first-time buyer gives up, checked it against Amazon, Noon and Jumia, asked an independent AI review tool for a second opinion, and turned it into prioritized stories, 3 sprints and 3 safe releases.

In one paragraph: The shop loses buyers who don't already trust it. They can't see the delivery date, the real total or the returns policy, and at the last step a pre-selected cash-on-delivery fee adds 7.00 the buyer never chose (+70% on our 10.00 test order). I propose 7 improvements, ranked with Itamar Gilad's ICE method so cheap, well-evidenced fixes ship first and the biggest bet is tested with buyers before it is built.
01

What is the goal, and why this one?

Business goal
More completed orders from the visitors the shop already has.
Product metric (north star)
Checkout completion rate: carts that become orders. It sits closest to revenue and the team can move it directly.
Revenue logic
Orders = visitors × add-to-cart × checkout completion. The first two cost marketing money; the third is lost money already in the cart.
Constraint
No access to real users or analytics, so evidence came from the live site, screenshots, competitors and an independent AI review. Stated openly throughout.
💡
My thinking I picked checkout completion over traffic or search because the buyer has already decided to buy. Losing her here is the most expensive loss, and the cheapest to win back.

Who are we solving for?

N

Nour, a doubtful first-time buyer (example persona)

Usually shops on Amazon. Found a lower price here but has never heard of this shop. Won't create an account for one purchase.

Needs before paying
Real total, delivery date and area, accepted payment, returns policy, proof the shop is real.
Leaves when
A cost appears late, a form blocks her, or the shop looks unfinished.
When I find a product cheaper than on Amazon but I don't know this shop, help me see the full price, delivery date and returns before I commit, so I can buy without feeling tricked.
💡
My thinking The brief offered "a user who wants to buy". I narrowed it to a buyer who doesn't trust the shop yet, because that person feels every gap: price, delivery, returns.

What is the true problem? Hypotheses tested

True problem
The shop asks a stranger to commit before showing what she needs to trust it, and then changes the price at the last step.
The moment it breaks
10.00 → 17.00
cart total vs confirm page, from a fee chosen by default
Flat fee, so the jump depends on the price
Book 10.00→ 17.00+70%
Item 100.00→ 107.00+7%
Laptop 1,590.00→ 1,597.00+0.4%
Small orders feel it most; on big orders it is the surprise, not the amount, that breaks trust.

06

What is it worth? (illustrative)

Back-of-envelope, example numbers
1,000 checkouts started a month
× 40% completion = 400 orders
Lift completion by 5 points → +50 orders
× 1,000 EGP average order = +50,000 EGP a month
Why now
Every week the price surprise stays, buyers who already chose a product are lost. The quick fixes cost days, not months.
In scope
Product page to thank-you page for guest buyers.
Out of scope (for now)
Business accounts, search filters, carrier integration, full order tracking.

The numbers are examples to show the reasoning; the real baseline comes from funnel tracking in sprint 1.

💡
My thinking Without real data, I show the reasoning, not a fake result: if the logic holds, the funnel baseline in sprint 1 fills in the numbers.
07

Which fixes first? Prioritized with ICE

How I ranked them
ICE = Impact × Confidence × Ease. Confidence is scored from evidence (Itamar Gilad's Confidence Meter), not from feeling.

Key call: the "real total" fix has the biggest impact but the least proof, so it is tested with buyers first and built in sprint 3, instead of dropped or rushed.
💡
My thinking Impact × effort alone overrates big ideas. Adding confidence from evidence moved the "real total" fix from 3rd to 5th, so we test it before we build it.

09

How will it ship?

Sprint 1 · release v2.1
Trust + measure
Returns promise, real content, guest-first, funnel tracking, test the real-total idea with buyers
Sprint 2 · release v2.2
No surprises
Delivery date on every product, payment methods early, checkout 6 → 5 steps
Sprint 3 · release v2.3
Real total
Full total in the cart, no-fee default, A/B test
Rollout
Each release is deployed switched off, then turned on for internal → 10% → 50% → 100% of visitors.
Rollback rule
If checkout completion falls more than 10% below the baseline for 2 days, the feature is switched off.
Capacity
48 points over 3 two-week sprints (example team of 4.5).
💡
My thinking Small releases behind feature flags: a bad idea costs a few days with 10% of visitors, not a month with everyone.

11

What could go wrong, and what I learned

Risk
Buyer tests show the price surprise matters less than expected.
Mitigation: the fix shrinks to the no-fee default; points move to other stories.
Risk
Delivery rules per area take longer.
Mitigation: show "Arrives by date" first, the countdown later.
Lesson
Writing stories before the evidence made the learning visible: 1 story disproved, 3 changed meaning, 2 new.
Lesson
A second opinion is worth it: the double-check caught that my walkthrough used a saved address, which hid a whole form.
Honest limit
No real users were interviewed. Every finding is expert or AI opinion until the sprint-1 buyer test and funnel data.
💡
My thinking The most useful finding came from being challenged: the double-check spotted that my walkthrough used a saved address. Ask to be proven wrong.
