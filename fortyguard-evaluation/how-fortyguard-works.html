FortyGuard evaluation · explainer · 20 Nov 2026

How FortyGuard turns heat into decisions, end to end

What hands-on testing found (21–22 Nov 2026). This page was written from FortyGuard's own materials before testing. Where testing disagreed: the free key had 1,000,000 one-time credits for one month, not 5M a month; the MCP worked in testing (11 tools, numbers identical to the API) despite the status page; cells are 100 m and 20 m fails; history starts 2021 and forecasts reach +13 h. The flow diagrams below still describe the design as documented. Full evidence: the evaluation report and evidence log.

FortyGuard's bet: temperature changes block by block, and those differences drive money, health and safety. Weather data gives you one number per area. FortyGuard models air temperature for each small tile, every hour, then sells it through three products: the Dashboard, the API and the MCP.

2How many inputs become one number: the physics, then the fusion

The air 2 m above a street gets warmer or cooler through a handful of physical processes. No single dataset sees them all. FortyGuard's job is to line the datasets up so a model can learn what the air temperature is, tile by tile. Move the clock to see which processes matter when.

🕐15:00Drag the clock: sunrise → noon → night

The scene shows textbook urban-heat physics, not FortyGuard's model. How each process maps to FortyGuard's inputs is on the cards: the input list is documented, and the mapping is assumed. The thermometer reading is illustrative.

1Sunlight arrives

The energy that drives everything, set by sun angle, season and cloud.Seen by: sun position (calculated), cloud from satellite and reanalysis. Solar radiation is also an API field. doc

2Light surfaces reflect it

A white roof sends much of the light back to the sky (high albedo), so less turns into heat.Seen by: satellite imagery (surface colour) and "materials" in their input list. doc mapping

3Dark surfaces absorb and store it

Asphalt and concrete soak up heat all day, like a battery.Seen by: land-cover share (built-up, roads, greenery) from the satellite endpoint. doc

4Stored heat comes back at night

Streets and walls give the heat back as infrared. Narrow streets between tall buildings trap it, so cities stay warm after sunset.Seen by: urban form, building height and street geometry. doc mapping

5Trees shade

Shade stops sunlight before it reaches the ground. FortyGuard cites up to 5 to 8 °C between shaded and exposed spots (Masdar).Seen by: greenery % from satellite and Street View segmentation. doc

6Wind carries heat away

Moving air mixes hot air out. Buildings slow it down, so a sheltered courtyard stays hotter.Seen by: weather stations and reanalysis (km scale). "Airflow" is part of their pitch. doc mapping

7Plants sweat and cool the air

Evapotranspiration turns heat into water vapour. It cools the air but raises humidity, which is why wet bulb matters.Seen by: greenery %, plus humidity from stations. Wet bulb is an output. doc

8People add heat

AC exhaust, traffic and industry. "Anthropogenic" is one of Heat Intelligence's 5 areas.Seen by: not stated. Possibly land use or traffic proxies. assumed

The aha: surfaces and shapes are known sharper than a tile, but the weather (wind, humidity, the regional temperature) is known only at the km scale. So the block-by-block differences come mostly from surface and geometry features. 3The resolution claims don't agree

4The time window isn't clear

Forecast: 12 h (V8) or 2 weeks (site). History: 2021 (V8) or 2019 (docs). Agents and apps can't plan around a limit they can't see. conflict

5People misread what the number means

It is 2 m air temperature, modelled. Yet 4 of 18 teams called it "surface" or "sensor" temperature, one of them inside an automated safety phone call. Field names need to say what the number is. doc

6The interfaces are inconsistent

api-key vs Bearer, FeatureCollection vs bare Polygon, "Completed" vs "succeeded", and rate limits "documented" but missing. conflict

7The status page is unreliable

The banner reads "Status unavailable", the MCP has no status label, and nothing below the customer boundary is monitored. doc

Teams added OpenMeteo fallbacks, caches and their own agent tools. Once the fallback kicks in, the hyperlocal promise quietly breaks. doc

The zoom floor conflictZooming past one tile shows no new detail. The tile size picked in step 3 (60–100 m) is the finest grain available. A "parcel-level" point query returns its tile's value, and one real user found the field "resolves near 1 km" (V4).
Zooming the map ≠ a new job assumedPanning and zooming a finished heatmap is free, because it redraws tiles already computed. Only a new area, time or tile size starts a job that costs credits. A heatmap costs 4,220 on Premium.
Reports narrow as you zoom in docWide views answer "where" (a map and stats). Close views answer "why" (land cover, Street View, Heat Intelligence). A point answers "what now" (wet bulb, comfort, AQI for one site and hour).

Where customer pain meets the product
  • Horizon. Utilities and HSE plan 1 to 14 days ahead. The API forecasts 12 h, so one team built its own 72 h model. gap
  • Resolution. Designers need the plaza scale, but the field "resolves near 1 km" for one user. gap
  • Batch and cost. Insurers and site teams score thousands of addresses at 2,900 credits a point. risk
  • Trust. Risk teams want error bars. No accuracy figure is published, and only 1 team showed confidence per cell. gap
  • Meaning. 4 teams labelled the number "surface" temperature, one inside a safety call. doc
  • Reliability. Agent builders lose their only door while the MCP is down. doc

10The aha, in five lines

It's a model factoryEach hour, data goes in and a grid of 2 m air temperatures comes out. Everything else is packaging.
With three doorsThe Dashboard is for eyes, the API for code and the MCP for agents. All three reach the same grid through the same gateway.
Automation sits on both sidesFortyGuard automates model runs, jobs, credits and alerts. Customers automate the decision: alerts, reroutes, shift changes and agents.
The weak points sit at the edgesThe precision claims sit at the model end, and outages and a stale status page sit at the delivery end. The middle, the async API, is the part that works.
Temperature is one ingredientEvery customer finishes the decision with outside data: census, assets, costs, routes, rules and design tools. The gaps they rebuild show FortyGuard's next products.
