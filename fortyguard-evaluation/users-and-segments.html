FortyGuard sells street-level heat as a decision input. Here is who buys it, when, and why.

Weather apps give one temperature for a whole area. FortyGuard models air temperature block by block (marketed as 20 m cells back to 2019 with forecasts up to 2 weeks; testing found 100 m tiles, history from 2021 and forecasts of hours, not weeks; US only) and packages it three ways: a Dashboard for people who look, an API for software that pulls, and an MCP for AI agents that ask. Why it exists: the forecast and the sidewalk disagree

Founded in Abu Dhabi in 2020, the team started on cooling materials and found they could not answer the first question: where, when and why does temperature become a problem? So they built their own data and models instead. Source

The pitch is that two points on the same site can differ by several degrees because of pavement, shade, buildings and airflow, and those differences drive cost, capacity, comfort and risk. A weather API "describes the atmosphere around a place"; FortyGuard adds geometry, materials and land cover to estimate the local thermal state of a site, asset or route. Source

What is different, in one line: the unit of analysis is a polygon, a building or a route, not a city. The outputs (heatmaps, hours above a threshold, ranked sites, a written "why is it hot" report, cooler routes) are shaped for a decision, not for a forecast screen. Inferred

Is there a niche?

Yes, where a heat difference between two nearby spots changes a decision worth real money: data-center and plant siting, masterplan and plaza design, portfolio risk, and heat-safe routing. Inferred

Signs it is real. Paid project work for named clients (DATS: 36 US AI data-center campuses; Ecopia: 40,110 features enriched in 6 days; TomTom route comparisons; Masdar City; MODON 100 layouts). NVIDIA Earth-2 collaboration and Microsoft Marketplace listing. CEO says US users grew 50% month over month, mostly through the free Dashboard. Source

Why it is hard. Inferred

  • Free substitutes: Landsat surface temperature, NWS HeatRisk and city heat-mapping campaigns cover the "where is it hot" question for many cities.
  • Usage is seasonal, so monthly self-serve plans risk churn every autumn.
  • Accuracy claims (115x, 2 m) need independent validation before risk teams trust them.
  • Free Dashboard users include homebuyers and parents: good for growth, little revenue.
  • US-only coverage today limits global logistics and insurance buyers.
