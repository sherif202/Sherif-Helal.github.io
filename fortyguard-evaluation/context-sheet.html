What FortyGuard says it does

Fact A "temperature operating system" (a foundation model family called LTM, Large Temperature Models) predicts air temperature 2 m above ground, hourly, at street-block scale. The Temperature API is the bridge between the models and products. The Dashboard is the front end on top of the API. (V1, V3)

Fact Inputs: proprietary ground-truth sensor data, satellite imagery, reanalysis (ERA5-type), weather stations, IoT, geographic and urban form, atmospheric and environmental layers. (V1, V3)

New Fact The API covers four data domains: temperature, comfort (wet bulb), air quality and land cover. Weather APIs are pitched as "zip code level" and FortyGuard as "parcel level", queryable at a single point. (V8)

Fact Positioning against alternatives: weather stations are sparse, satellite LST is surface not air, reanalysis is coarse (25 km "one value per city"). FortyGuard claims to fill the street-level, hourly, 2 m gap. (V3)

Repeated lines

  • "Democratize the use of temperature", "for engineers and non-engineers" (V1, V2)
  • "The truth of temperature", "temperature at the human level, not the atmosphere" (V1, V2, V4)
  • Collecting urban heat data is "a broken industry", slow and expensive (V1)
  • "From the fingertip of their desktop", replacing site visits (V1)
  • Shipping cadence: parameters added "bi-weekly", intelligence sources "weekly", "something new almost every week" (V1, V2)

  • New Heat is framed as a health and productivity problem: 235,000 US heat-related ER visits a year, lost worker productivity "doubling by 2030" (CEO, V11)

The hackathon

Fact Two weeks, final submission 30 August (2026, per V4), video submission required, support through Slack and a hackathon email, API documentation shared with participants and mentors, winners to be published. US coverage only. (V4, V7)

Fact Problem set: reduce urban heat, framed as cooling assets, growth sites, streets or neighbourhoods. Teams were told to combine FortyGuard temperature data with open data (demographics, infrastructure, land cover). (V4, V3)

Fact Mentor sessions: FortyGuard data science (V3), a landscape architect's DC case study (V4), NVIDIA on CUDA-X, NIMs and Earth-2 weather models (V5), pitching to judges (V6), product-market fit by an ex-BreezoMeter founder (V7). Judging stressed real problem, real user, clear story.

New Fact Rules and tracks. Using the FortyGuard API was compulsory and other open data was welcome. Judging favoured "commercial value" over engineering, and projects had to be deployed live (Render, Vercel, GitHub Pages) and keep calling the API so judges could test them. AI coding tools were allowed if declared. Named tracks included resilient cities and infrastructure, future buildings and energy, and real estate. Autodesk offered a 30-day Forma trial for building site-design extensions that pull in FortyGuard data. (V8, V9)

Fact Which product teams used: only the Temperature API is mentioned. MCP is never mentioned. Changed What teams built is now covered by 8 demo videos (see Team projects). FortyGuard's engineer expected most teams to build cooler-routes apps (V8). 6 of 18 did. New One demo names "Track 03, industrial and enterprise" (V18).

Pain points that surfaced

  • Changed Land cover is only partly in the API. The V3 Q&A said the API "doesn't provide that directly" and sent teams to Sentinel or Landsat. V8 shows the satellite endpoint returns land-cover percentages for an area. Two different answers in the same hackathon point to unclear docs.
  • US-only coverage made it hard for non-US teams to validate ideas. (V7 Q&A)
  • Coarser than advertised in practice: the DC mentor said his field "resolves near 1 km", so a street corner is below what the data shows, and tripling trees moved air temperature by hundredths of a degree. (V4)
  • Flat fields: a downtown basin read about 2/3 °C end to end, too flat to act on. (V4)
  • Empty tiles over water: about 18 km² of the DC study came back empty. (V4)
  • Threshold saturation: on a clear day 95% of tiles were above 30 °C for 11+ hours; he had to re-pull at 33 °C. (V4)
  • Data-alignment traps FortyGuard itself warns about: UTC vs local time, whether "14:00" is the start or end of the hour, missing hours, air vs surface temperature, units. (V3)
  • Q&A questions were moved to email and Slack. (V4)
  • New Unclear rules mid-event: the submission criteria were "coming in a day or two" while teams were already building, and participants were asked to stop "spamming" questions (V8, V9).
  • New Gaps in the data: FortyGuard advised teams to "rely on interpolation" for missing or inconsistent values (V8).
  • New Quota worries: teams were warned that free hosting tiers "die down" and to keep their endpoints live for judging. Inference This puts sustained load on the API right after the event, matching the start of the 4 to 21 Sep down days.

  • All API components at 98.23 to 98.24%. Dashboard 98.27%, Alerts 99.10%, Auth 98.72%, MCP 78.00%.
  • Down days: 7, 17, 18, 21 Sep, then 9 and 11 to 18 Nov (7 of the last 10 days). The Dashboard was also down on 2 Oct.
  • On 18 Nov, about 100 automatic incidents fired between 04:00 and 09:04 UTC (11 pm to 4 am US Eastern), each lasting 2 to 49 minutes, across every component at the same moments.
  • Incident text: "failed requests are safe to retry, and only completed requests consume credits".

Inconsistencies Fact

  • History says "No incidents recorded" in September and October, but the bars show 4 down days in September and one in October.
  • The open MCP incident (since 14 Oct) is not in the history at all.
  • MCP shows "No data" for today and for most of 15 Oct to 10 Nov, yet the incident is still "Investigating".
  • The history list stops at exactly about 100 entries, all from 18 Nov. Inference It is capped, so Nov 11 to 17 incidents are hidden.
  • Incidents "open and resolve automatically". There are no human updates, causes or post-mortems.

What it says about scale Inference

  • Every component fails together, to the minute. That points to one shared dependency (gateway, auth, database or the monitor itself), not per-service load.
  • 98.2% over about 78 monitored days is roughly 30+ hours of downtime. That is well below the 99.9% a B2B API usually promises.
  • The failures happen in November, overnight in the US, off-season. They are not caused by heat-wave demand. So a summer heat-wave spike, when cities and utilities need the data most, is untested and higher risk.
  • Short 2 to 3 minute flaps may be monitor noise. Either way, a buyer reading this page sees instability.

Against the earlier users map

Confirmed

  • City heat officers, planners, landscape architects, public health and homebuyers or parents as users (V1). A landscape studio really is building on the API (V4).
  • Air temperature at 2 m; US-only (V1, V3, V7).
  • Free Dashboard with a paid tier; credit-based API (V1, S).
  • The "why is it hot" written report exists as Heat Intelligence (V2).
  • NVIDIA relationship: an NVIDIA mentor and judge at the hackathon (V5).
  • New Masdar City and the Abu Dhabi origin (V11).
  • New The "AI agent builders" segment is real. 14 of 18 teams built AI layers and 5 built tool-calling agents (V12–V29).
  • New Data centres, utilities, insurance, logistics and cold chain, and real estate as API segments: FortyGuard built demo apps for each (V8).
  • New Cooler routes: FortyGuard demoed a cool-routes app built on the API (V8). It may be a demo rather than a product feature.

Contradicted or complicated

  • 20 m grid: the videos show 60 to 100 m tiles, and a user saw about 1 km.
  • 9 MCP tools, validated clients: the status page shows MCP effectively never up.
  • Peak load in heat waves (an inference in the map): outages so far are off-season and overnight, which points to fragility rather than load.
  • Changed Utilities, insurance, logistics as main API users: FortyGuard does target these (V8), but only through its own demos. No customers are named.
  • New Forecasts up to 2 weeks, back to 2019: V8 says 12 hours and 2021.
  • The map leaves out Heat Monitoring & Alerts and the segmentation APIs listed on the status page.

Not covered by these sources

  • DATS, Ecopia, TomTom, Masdar City, MODON case studies
  • $79 / $289 pricing and 115x accuracy claim
  • 52B data points

These still rest on the website and press, so treat them as claims.
