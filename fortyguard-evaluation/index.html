Product evaluation · 21–22 November 2026

FortyGuard: the data and the AI assistant are ready. The dashboard isn't yet.

FortyGuard models street-level air temperature, tile by tile and hour by hour, and sells it three ways: a Temperature Dashboard for people, a Temperature API for software and a Temperature MCP for AI agents. As part of my application for the FortyGuard Product Internship, I tested all three the way a new customer would, logged every check, and turned what I found into root causes, ten prioritised fixes and one product idea.

What I found

The numbers underneath are sound: the API, the MCP and the dashboard return the same temperature for the same place and hour. Trust breaks in the dashboard, which draws, labels and reports those numbers in ways that look wrong.

  1. Small differences look dramatic. A 0.3 °C spread is painted from deep red to deep blue.
  2. The first demo shows an impossible number. Los Angeles in January at 101.9 °F, which FortyGuard's own generated report calls implausible.
  3. The app doesn't say what is happening or what it costs. Progress freezes at 30%, finished reports look missing, and no price appears before paid actions.
  4. Docs and product disagree in 13 places on limits, plans and tools, and the API key only exists in an email.

How I worked

ScopeTemperature Dashboard, Temperature API and Temperature MCP, on a free trial account (US coverage).
MethodA written plan with hypotheses, then 67 planned steps (64 run), 16 recorded dashboard walkthroughs, 27 timed API calls and MCP prompts in Claude Code. Every result went into the evidence log.
LensesNielsen's 10 usability heuristics, a documentation scorecard, Lighthouse for speed, and reliability, scalability and maintainability from Designing Data-Intensive Applications.
Cost67,480 trial credits, spent only where a test needed it.
LimitsTwo days, one trial account and no access to real customers or usage data. Usage patterns and segment mappings are my own reading, and are marked as such.
